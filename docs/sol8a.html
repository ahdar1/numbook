Week 8a

1.2. Fixed cost and variable cost

Problem

You sell a product with a price of £20 per unit. The variable cost of the product is £10 per unit. The fixed costs associated with the product is £20,000. How many products should you sell to achieve break-even?

Answer:

\(Break-even \ quantity = \frac{Fixed \ cost}{Contribution}\)

\(Contribution = Selling \ Price - Variable \ Cost\)

\(Break-even \ quantity = \frac{20000}{20-10}\) = 2000

You must sell 2000 units to achieve break-even.

Break-even analysis

Problem

You sell a product with a price of £20 per unit. The variable cost of the product is £10 per unit. The fixed costs associated with the product is £20,000. How many products should you sell to achieve break-even?

Answer:

Fixed cost = £20000

Selling price = £20

Variable cost per product = £10

\[\begin{align} Contribution &= Selling \; Price - Variable \; Cost \\ &= £20 - £10 = £10 \end{align}\]

\[\begin{align} BE \; quantity &= \frac{Fixed \ cost}{Contribution} \\ &= \frac{£20000}{£10} \\ &= 2000 \end{align}\]

Thus, you should sell 2000 units.

Exercises

  1. A company’s variable costs per product are £5. The daily fixed costs are £100. If the product is sold at £10, calculate the break-even quantity.

Answer

\[\begin{align} Contribution &= Selling \; Price - Variable \; Cost \\ &= £10 - £5 = £5 \end{align}\]

\[\begin{align} BE \; quantity &= \frac{Fixed \ cost}{Contribution} \\ &= \frac{£100}{£5} \\ &= 20 \end{align}\]

  1. If you increase the selling price by 5%, calculate the new break-even quantity.

Answer

New selling price = \(105 \% \times £10 = 10.5\)

\[\begin{align} Contribution &= Selling \; Price - Variable \; Cost \\ &= £10.5 - £5 = £5.5 \end{align}\]

\[\begin{align} BE \; quantity &= \frac{Fixed \ cost}{Contribution} \\ &= \frac{£100}{£5.5} \\ &= 18.18 \end{align}\]

The BE quantity is 18.18. You can not sell partial units. You may round up to the next whole number (if you round down, you will incur a profit loss). Thus, you must sell at least 19 units.
